# Figure legends

These legends describe the seven replacement/redraw figures. Figure numbers refer to this redraw set; they are not yet inserted into the current one-main-figure manuscript.

**Figure 1 | Discovery-cohort transcriptomic comparisons.** (a) Descriptive PCA of the processed expression matrix for 58 participants: 10 healthy controls (HC), 10 trauma controls (TC) and 38 spinal cord injury (SCI) participants. All 17,498 nonconstant genes were centered and scaled before PCA. No group ellipses are shown. (b) SCI-minus-HC differential-expression results; dashed lines mark log2 fold changes of −1 and +1 and BH q = 0.05. (c,d) Counts require both BH q < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 1. In c, “Other” means not passing both thresholds and does not mean statistically nonsignificant by BH alone. In d, positive direction is toward the first named group in each contrast. Comparisons use the saved historical processed-expression tables. PCA positions and DEG counts are descriptive and do not establish an SCI-specific effect beyond trauma.

**Figure 2 | Fixed immune gene-set comparisons.** CAMERA competitive tests for eight revision-fixed immune Gene Ontology terms under M0 (group), M1 (group + age + sex) and M2 (M1 + injury severity score + traumatic brain injury + prior central nervous system pathology), using the same 43 participants (36 SCI and 7 TC). Each model's BH q values are corrected across all 4,032 tested GO sets; all displayed terms and all three models are shown. Triangles encode competitive direction and color encodes model. The q = 0.05 line is a reference threshold. Competitive direction and significance are not measures of pathway activity or biological effect size.

**Figure 3 | Frozen 30-gene discovery signature.** Row-wise z-score display of the 15 myeloid/innate-associated and 15 lymphocyte/T-cell-associated genes across all 58 discovery-cohort participants, grouped as HC (n = 10), TC (n = 10) and SCI (n = 38). Color saturates beyond ±3; no observations or gene values were removed. The group labels contributed to gene selection, so this same-cohort heatmap is descriptive and is not independent validation.

**Figure 4 | Gene-clustering diagnostics.** (a) Mean silhouette by the tested number of gene clusters k; the largest value occurs at k = 2. (b) Gap statistic with simulation standard errors; the one-standard-error rule selects the upper search boundary, k = 6. (c) Mean best-matched Jaccard membership and empirical 2.5th–97.5th percentile ranges across 200 participant-stratified bootstrap resamples for each fixed k. The diagnostics condition on the previously selected universe of 2,509 genes and 58 participants. Stable membership at a specified k does not establish that k is optimal. Clustering units are gene profiles, not participants.

**Figure 5 | Longitudinal frozen-score patterns.** (a) Participant-linked myeloid/innate-associated, lymphocyte/T-cell-associated and secondary balance scores across acute, 3-month, 6-month and 12-month visits in 10 SCI participants. Each score has all 37 observations: 10, 10, 10 and 7 at the four visits, respectively. Lines connect repeated observations from the same participant. Full-count coverage is 15+15; nine uninjured controls supplied gene-reference parameters. (b) All nine saved time-only random-intercept contrasts (score ~ visit + (1 | participant)) for each later visit minus acute, with pointwise 95% confidence intervals. Models used REML and Satterthwaite inference. Raw P values and the saved BH9 q values accompany the source table. Estimates are re-displayed from the saved analysis, not refit for figure production. The secondary balance is the difference between component means. The scores summarize bulk-expression patterns and do not establish immune or neurological recovery.

**Figure 6 | Frozen-score distributions in the discovery cohort.** (a) Participant positions using the frozen myeloid/innate-associated score and lymphocyte/T-cell-associated score. Each component averages the frozen genes' z scores using the TC reference parameters. (b) Component distributions for the same participants. Each point is one participant; boxes show the median and interquartile range, and whiskers extend to the most extreme observations within 1.5 times the interquartile range. There are 10 HC, 10 TC and 38 SCI participants. Group labels contributed to signature selection. This selected coordinate space is descriptive: no post-selection tests, disease-progression axis or participant transition is inferred.

**Figure 7 | Additional-cohort assessment with partial sample reuse.** (a) Supplied frozen-score values in GSE293559: 36 SCI participants and 9 uninjured controls. Symbols distinguish participants reused from GSE226238. Boxes show medians and IQRs, with whiskers extending to the most extreme observations within 1.5 times the IQR; all individuals are overlaid. Coverage is 14/15 genes in each component; TLR5 and ITK are absent. (b–d) Saved Hedges g estimates for the myeloid/innate-associated, lymphocyte/T-cell-associated and secondary balance scores, respectively, across the full sample (36/9), exclusion of reused SCI participants (26/9), same-platform restriction (31/9) and same-platform restriction plus exclusion of reused SCI participants (21/9). Points show g and bars show participant-bootstrap 95% confidence intervals. Excluding reused SCI participants retains all nine reused controls. The original supplied score transformation was held fixed and was not independently reconstructed. Two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum P values and separate within-scope BH3 q values are supplied in Figure7_reuse_platform_effects.csv; the tests concern rank distributions, not Hedges g. No trauma-control comparison is available.
